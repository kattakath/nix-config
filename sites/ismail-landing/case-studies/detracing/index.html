Case study — methodology

De-tracing: recovering the design system inside an auto-traced logo

A client logo arrived as auto-traced vector soup: 92 wobbly segments, no symmetry, stroke widths wandering 30–37 units. Instead of redrawing it, I ran an analysis-by-synthesis loop — measure, fit closed-form, render, diff, repeat — with an AI vision model as the critic. Out came 14 primitives, 15 constants, four motion equations, and a drift certificate proving the residual is 1.5% and every unit of it intentional.

Analysis by synthesis, run by hand and critic

02 — The loop

The method hypothesizes a parametric model, renders it, compares against the source raster, and lets the residual drive the next update. Three properties distinguish it from gradient-based vectorization (DiffVG-class methods): fits are closed-form, so the output is interpretable constants rather than Bézier soup; model selection happens inside the loop — a hypothesis can be rejected and replaced, not merely tuned; and the critic is perceptual, catching failures that pixel metrics structurally cannot.

Fifteen constants were hiding in the wobble

03 — What measurement found

Cross-sections of the traced walls fit straight lines with slopes 0.5506 and 0.5510 — the designer meant 0.55. The midline sat at 255.3 on a 512 canvas — they meant 256. Perpendicular stroke thickness clustered at 32; letter arm lines landed on 370 / 427.5 / 485 with the S's lobes at radius 28.75 and the C's lobe at exactly twice that. The original had a design system; the tracing had buried it. The rebuild's entire geometry is those recovered constants plus tangency math — corner fillets solved exactly, symmetry by mirrored coordinates rather than by drawing.

fillet center: x_c = c − k(y_b − R) + R·√(1+k²) r_C = 2·r_S = 57.5

Three hypotheses died. That is the point.

04 — Falsifications

Gradient methods tune a hypothesis; they cannot reject one. This loop did, three times — each caught by measurement or by looking, each replacing a model class, not a parameter:

Falsified 01 · bottom topology

Modeled: one continuous bottom stroke. Measured: the source's bottom edge terminates into the letters — the left piece becomes the S, the right piece rises as the I, and the gap around the C cuts through to white. The rebuild's topology was restructured, not re-tuned.

Falsified 02 · the lip "curl"

Modeled: an arc with a descending tail. Row-profile measurement showed the lip occupies exactly one stroke-width vertically — it is a horizontal bar with a round cap; the "curl" was the cap's curvature. The model class changed from arc to segment.

Falsified 03 · containment proof

A clip-path "proof" checked the arc midspan and missed that its own sharp corners leaked 42 units past the stroke. The render caught it; the corrected clip follows the fillet arcs, and the re-proof samples 3,000 boundary points instead of arguing about them.

The failure catalog is the finding

05 — What the numbers missed

Three defects passed every numerical assertion and fell only to a vision check in the real rendering context: a WebKit thumbnail bug that dropped an element cairosvg rendered fine; blind probe points that sampled gaps in the geometry and reported success; and a CSS-class refactor that was leaner and degraded catastrophically in CSS-less rasterizers. The generalizable result: loops with derivable ground truth close themselves; perceptual-quality loops need a vision oracle — and knowing where that boundary sits is worth more than pretending it isn't there.

That boundary is directly load-bearing for production document-AI: OCR training loops can self-evaluate (render → rasterize → recognize → compare gives free ground truth), while layout fidelity, rendering QA, and "does this extraction look right" sit on the oracle side. The same architecture I run for clients — automated metrics for what is derivable, model-vision review for what is perceptual.

Motion and theming, same discipline

06 — Also recovered

The rebuilt mark animates: a seamless 4-second loop where bubbles detach from the liquid under constant-flux inflation (r ∝ t1/3), rise under Stokes drag toward a terminal velocity that equals the conveyor's handoff speed — the physics lands the animation constraint for free — and ring with a damped quadrupole wobble. The liquid surface is a translating sine strip: a traveling wave is its own shape in uniform translation, so the loop seals by periodicity, not by careful keyframing. Theming collapsed to a single input: currentColor drives structure, liquid strata, and effects through masks that encode alpha, not color — one asset, every theme, verified by pixel-sampling through the actual CSS inheritance path.
