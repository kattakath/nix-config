Multi-GPU GenAI inference, productionized.

Case study

Silver Creek Insights · Principal Software Engineer · Aug 2024 — Mar 2026

I architected the GenAI inference infrastructure — multi-GPU serving on vLLM and Kubernetes — that fed 80+ app vendors, cutting inference cost ~25% and latency ~40% on that platform, with the governance, observability and delivery spine to run it in production.

vLLM Kubernetes Multi-GPU serving Snowflake agents Strands Agents HITL / RL IaC · CI/CD

Problem

01 — The mandate

A shared GenAI capability served a fleet of downstream products — the optimized image-manipulation pipelines alone were consumed by 80+ app vendors. On that kind of platform, multi-GPU inference is the dominant cost and latency line, and it has to stay fast while agents reach into governed data. The job was to make multi-GPU serving cheaper and faster, keep access control and data-residency intact, and ship it as production infrastructure rather than a notebook demo.

Constraints

02 — What the design had to respect

Many vendors, one platform

The image-manipulation pipelines were shared by 80+ app vendors — the serving layer had to scale to a broad, multi-consumer load rather than a single application.

Governed data, by rule

The AI agents built within Snowflake carried granular access controls and data-residency compliance — access and placement were compliance requirements, not preferences.

Structured and unstructured

The multi-agent orchestration prototype (Strands Agents) had to reason across both structured and unstructured datasets, so the design couldn't assume one clean data shape.

Production bar, not demo

Agentic-AI pipelines were productionized against scalability, observability, security, IaC and CI/CD — the whole thing had to be operable and reproducible, not a one-off.

Humans in the loop

HITL AI/ML and reinforcement-learning workflows needed interactive review UIs — human judgement was part of the pipeline, so the system had to expose it, not hide it.

Serving core

vLLM on Kubernetes

Multi-GPU inference serving is the heart of the platform — the layer where the ~25% cost and ~40% latency reduction (on that platform) was won. Kubernetes carried the scaling and scheduling.

Shared pipelines

Highly-optimized image-manipulation pipelines ran as shared infrastructure, consumed by 80+ distinct app vendors rather than rebuilt per product.

Governed agents

Snowflake AI agents

Specialized AI agents engineered inside Snowflake with granular access controls and data-residency compliance, so governed data stayed governed.

Orchestration (prototype)

Strands multi-agent

A multi-agent orchestration prototype on Strands Agents, spanning structured and unstructured datasets — deliberately scoped as a prototype, not yet production.

Human in the loop

Interactive UIs for human-in-the-loop AI/ML and reinforcement-learning workflows, putting human judgement into the pipeline as a first-class step.

Platform plane

Every backend rode the same productionization spine — scalability, observability, security, infrastructure-as-code and CI/CD — so the pipelines were operable and reproducible.

Tradeoffs

05 — What I bought, and what it cost

Self-measured, single platform

The ~25% / ~40% gains are measured before/after in one production environment — honest, but not an independently-audited benchmark. I keep the "on that platform" hedge attached rather than let the numbers travel further than the measurement does.

Prototype, not production

The Strands multi-agent orchestration was scoped as a prototype across structured and unstructured data. That bought fast exploration of the orchestration space; the cost is that it didn't ride the same production spine as the serving pipelines — yet.

Governance narrows placement

Granular access controls and data-residency compliance on the Snowflake agents constrain where data and computation can live. That trades some architectural freedom for compliance you can actually stand behind.

Humans cost throughput

HITL and reinforcement-learning review UIs put a person in the loop. That adds latency and throughput cost against a fully-automated path — paid deliberately for label quality and a cleaner training signal.

What I'd do differently

06 — Next time
Upgrade the grade

Benchmark, don't just self-measure

I'd stand up a reproducible cost/latency benchmark in CI so the ~25% / ~40% numbers can move from self-measured to independently verifiable — the same figures, but portable off that one platform.

Graduate the prototype

Strands onto the production spine

I'd promote the multi-agent orchestration from prototype onto the same observability, IaC and CI/CD spine the serving pipelines already ride, instead of leaving it a separate track.

Close the loop

Feed HITL back into serving

I'd tighten the path from the HITL/RL review UIs back into the served models, so human corrections become a measured, first-class improvement signal rather than an offline step.
